Criticism & Craft

Essays

Short critical and personal essays on the recurring images, religious interpretation, governing methods, and working beliefs behind the fiction, poetry, and teaching of James Mulhern.

An Editor’s Note

On this series

These nineteen essays read James Mulhern’s work the way a careful reader would — following a single image, method, or conviction across stories, poems, and the classroom until it declares its own logic. Six are close readings of the fiction and its imagery: fire and water, the sacred hidden inside shabby, ordinary detail, a scholarly reading of the Assumption of Mary as a key to “Assumptions,” and the sacrament of communion running through the classroom, the fiction, and the poems. One is a full-length study of the short story collection All We Ever Needed, tracing its themes, motifs, character archetypes, allusions, and craft across all eight stories. Four extend the same close-reading method to the poetry of Crossings, to the allusions inside Molly Bonamici, to the recurring geography of the whole body of work, and to four comparative studies placing Mulhern’s fiction and poetry beside James Joyce, Flannery O’Connor, and Raymond Carver. One turns the method on the writer himself, asking what psychological research on permeable mental boundaries can and cannot explain about a lifelong practice of recurrence. Two are the author’s own account of what he believes writing, teaching, and communion are for. Four newer essays turn to narrative technique directly: unreliable child narrators in Molly Bonamici and Give Them Unquiet Dreams, the unlicensed confessors who recur across the stories, the genetic evidence of Mulhern’s own habit of redrafting a scene more than once in public, and the craft function of Aiden Glencar’s second sight.
